The Science Behind the Forecast

How we predict wave pool temperatures

1. Pool Temperature Model

The pool temperature is advanced in time using a single-layer (well-mixed) energy balance with a forward Euler integration scheme:

T_pool_new = T_pool + Q_total × Δt / (ρ × d × c_p)

where ρ = 1000 kg/m³ (water density), d = pool effective depth (m), c_p = 4186 J/kg/K (specific heat), Δt = time step (s)

The total surface heat flux combines five components:

Q_total = Q_solar + Q_lw_net + Q_conv + Q_evap + Q_ground

2. Absorbed Solar Radiation

Shortwave energy absorbed at the water surface after reflection:

Q_solar = (1 − α) × G_s

α = 0.06 (shortwave albedo of open water), G_s = daily mean solar irradiance (W/m²) from Open-Meteo API

3. Net Longwave Radiation

Difference between downwelling atmospheric emission and upwelling emission from the pool surface:

Q_lw_net = ε_sky × σ × T_air⁴ − ε_water × σ × T_pool⁴

σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W/m²/K⁴ (Stefan-Boltzmann), ε_water = 0.97

Sky emissivity is estimated using the Brutsaert (1975) formula based on near-surface vapour pressure e_a (hPa) and air temperature T_air (K):

ε_sky = 1.24 × (e_a / T_air)^(1/7)    [Brutsaert 1975]

4. Sensible Heat (Convection)

Turbulent heat exchange between the air and water surface using the McAdams (1954) bulk-transfer coefficient:

Q_conv = h_c × (T_air − T_pool)

h_c = 5.7 + 3.8 × U    [W m⁻² K⁻¹]

U = wind speed (m/s). Positive Q_conv warms the pool when air is warmer than water.

5. Evaporative Heat Loss

Latent heat removed by evaporation using a bulk atmospheric transfer approach (Penman 1948 / Monteith):

Q_evap = −L × f(U) × (e_s(T_pool) − e_a)

f(U) ≈ C_E × U    [kg m⁻² s⁻¹ kPa⁻¹]

L = 2.45×10⁶ J/kg (latent heat of vaporisation), e_s(T_pool) = saturation vapour pressure at pool temperature (kPa), e_a = actual vapour pressure of air (kPa). This flux is nearly always negative (heat loss).

6. Ground Heat Exchange

Conductive heat transfer through the pool slab and soil to undisturbed earth, modelled as two resistances in series:

Q_ground = −U_bottom × (T_pool − T_ground)

U_bottom = 1 / (R_concrete + R_soil)
R_concrete = L_slab / k_concrete    [m²K/W]
R_soil     = d_soil  / k_soil       [m²K/W]

k_concrete ≈ 1.4 W/m/K, L_slab = 0.305 m (1 ft). k_soil varies by location (0.35–1.5 W/m/K). T_ground = annual mean deep-soil temperature (°C).

7. Data Sources

Weather and solar forcing data are fetched automatically once per day from free public APIs:

  • NWS API — weather.gov — Hourly air temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, and wind direction forecasts for each pool location.
  • Open-Meteo API — open-meteo.com — Daily total shortwave solar radiation (MJ/m²/day). No API key required.

All retrieved data is stored in a local SQLite database for persistence and used as the thermal model's initial condition on subsequent runs.

8. Wetsuit Recommendation Guide

Suit recommendations are assigned based on forecasted pool water temperature:

Water Temp (°F) Recommended Suit Notes
≥ 75°F 🩳 Boardshorts Warm water — no thermal protection needed
69 – 74°F 👕 Top / Shorty Light spring suit or rash guard
63 – 68°F 🩱 2mm Springsuit / 3/2 Full Suit Moderate thermal protection
58 – 62°F 🥶 4/3 Full Suit Cool water — full suit recommended
52 – 57°F 🥶❄️ 4/3 Full Suit + Booties Cold water — add booties for foot warmth
< 52°F 🧊 5/4 Full Suit + Hood + Booties Very cold — maximum insulation required
